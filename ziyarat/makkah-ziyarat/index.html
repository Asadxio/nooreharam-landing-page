Makkah Ziyarat Places & Sacred Sites

A spiritual and historical journey through the birthplace of Islam, the sites of divine revelation, and the landmarks of Hajj.

Makkah Mukarramah is enveloped in divine history. Every mountain, valley, and courtyard has witnessed pivotal moments in the life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and earlier prophets including Ibrahim (A.S) and Ismail (A.S). Below is an authentic guide to the prominent Ziyarat sites included in NOOR-E-HARAM pilgrimage tours.

1. Jabal al-Nour & Ghar Hira (Cave of Hira)

Rising majestically in the northeast of Makkah, Jabal al-Nour (the Mountain of Light) holds the sacred Cave of Hira. Here, in solitary reflection, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ received the very first revelation of the Holy Quran through Angel Jibril (A.S): "Iqra bismi Rabbikalladhee khalaq" (Read in the name of your Lord who created).

2. Jabal Thawr & Ghar Thawr (Cave of Thawr)

Located to the south of Makkah, Mount Thawr contains the cave in which the Prophet ﷺ and his loyal companion Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (R.A) sheltered for three days during the historic Hijrah migration to Madinah. It is the blessed sanctuary mentioned in Surah At-Tawbah (9:40): "When the two were in the cave, he said to his companion: Do not grieve, indeed Allah is with us."

3. The Sacred Stations of Hajj: Mina, Arafat & Muzdalifah

  • Mina: The valley of white tents where pilgrims stay during Hajj and perform the symbolic stoning of the Jamarat, recalling Ibrahim's (A.S) rejection of Satan.
  • Plains of Arafat & Jabal al-Rahmah: The Mount of Mercy where the Prophet ﷺ delivered his historic Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada) and where the crucial pillar of Hajj takes place.
  • Muzdalifah (Al-Mash'ar al-Haram): The open plain where pilgrims spend the night under the stars in prayer and collect pebbles for the Jamarat.

4. Jannat al-Mu'alla Cemetery

The historic cemetery in Makkah where many revered members of the Prophet's ﷺ noble family and companions are buried, including the Mother of the Believers, Sayyidatuna Khadijah al-Kubra (R.A), and the Prophet's grandfather Abdul Muttalib.
