Madinah Ziyarat Places & Prophetic Landmarks

A spiritual guide to the holy mosques, battlegrounds, and sanctuaries of Madinah Munawwarah, the illuminated city of peace.

Madinah Munawwarah holds a unique place in the heart of every believer. It is the city that welcomed the Prophet ﷺ with open arms (Al-Ansar), where the first Islamic society was established, and where the Prophet ﷺ rests. NOOR-E-HARAM leads structured, respectful tours of the key prophetic sites in Madinah.

1. Masjid Quba — The First Mosque of Islam

Masjid Quba is the very first mosque built in Islamic history. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ laid its foundation stones upon arriving in the outskirts of Madinah during the Hijrah. Praying two voluntary Rakahs here is equated to the reward of performing a complete Umrah according to Sahih Hadith.

2. Mount Uhud & Shuhada Uhud (Martyrs of Uhud)

Rising north of Madinah, Mount Uhud was declared by the Prophet ﷺ: "Uhud is a mountain that loves us and we love it." At its base lies the resting place of 70 noble martyrs of the Battle of Uhud (3 AH), including the Prophet's beloved uncle, Sayyiduna Hamzah ibn Abdul Muttalib (R.A), the "Lion of Allah".

3. Masjid al-Qiblatayn (Mosque of the Two Qiblas)

This historic mosque is the site where the Prophet ﷺ was commanded by Allah (SWT) during prayer to redirect the Qiblah from Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem) toward the Holy Kaaba in Makkah (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:144). Pilgrims can observe the architectural markers honoring this divine transition.

4. The Seven Mosques & Battle of the Trench (Khandaq)

A cluster of historical small mosques situated along the western foot of Mount Sala', where the Muslim army led by the Prophet ﷺ dug a defensive trench around Madinah in 5 AH upon the brilliant counsel of Salman al-Farsi (R.A).

5. Jannat al-Baqi (Baqi al-Gharqad)

Adjacent to Al-Masjid an-Nabawi, this blessed cemetery contains the resting places of approximately 10,000 companions (Sahaba), wives of the Prophet (Ummahat al-Mu'minin), and his noble children and family members.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can women enter Jannat al-Baqi?

In accordance with Saudi regulatory arrangements, men can enter the cemetery pathways during designated morning hours after Fajr and Asr prayers. Women can offer Salam from the designated viewing platforms along the outer perimeter of the Baqi wall.
