41 Sacred Ziyarat Places Catalog

The complete, itemized historical register of the 41 blessed landmarks in Makkah al-Mukarramah and Al-Madinah al-Munawwarah. Walk through the authentic footsteps of the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ), the noble Sahabah, and decisive moments in Islamic civilization.

The Spiritual Dimension of Islamic Ziyarat

Performing Ziyarat is not mere sightseeing. It is an act of spiritual contemplation (Tadabbur) that breathes life into the verses of the Holy Quran and the Hadith literature. When a pilgrim stands at Mount Uhud, visits the first mosque of Islam in Quba, or gazes up at the peak of Jabal al-Noor where the angel Jibril commanded "Iqra!", history transforms into faith.

Below is the complete, documented catalog of the 41 Holy Sites covered across our comprehensive pilgrimage tours.

Part 1: The 18 Blessed Sites of Makkah Al-Mukarramah

  • 1. Jabal al-Noor & Cave of Hira (Ghar-e-Hira): The mountain of light where the Prophet (ﷺ) engaged in solitary worship and received the very first Quranic revelation (Surah Al-Alaq).
  • 2. Jabal Thawr & Cave of Thawr: The mountain where the Prophet (ﷺ) and Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (RA) took refuge for three nights during the historic Hijrah migration to Madinah.
  • 3. Plain of Mina: The tent city of Hajj where the Prophet (ﷺ) camped and where pilgrims stone the Jamarat.
  • 4. Plain of Arafat & Jabal al-Rahmah: The Mount of Mercy where the Prophet delivered his historic Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada) to over 100,000 companions.
  • 5. Muzdalifah (Mash'ar al-Haram): The open plain under the desert sky where pilgrims combine Maghrib and Isha prayers and gather pebbles for the Jamarat.
  • 6. Masjid Nimra: The landmark mosque located at the boundary of Arafat where the annual Hajj sermon is delivered.
  • 7. Jamarat Complex: The multi-tier bridge housing the three stone pillars (Al-Aqaba, Al-Wusta, Al-Sughra) symbolizing Ibrahim's (AS) rejection of Satan.
  • 8. Jannat al-Mu'alla: Makkah's ancient cemetery where the Mother of the Believers, Hazrat Khadija (RA), the Prophet’s son Qasim, and grandfather Abdul Muttalib are buried.
  • 9. Masjid Aisha (Masjid at-Taneem): The primary Miqat station outside the Haram boundary where pilgrims enter the state of Ihram for additional Umrahs.
  • 10. Masjid al-Jinn: The historical mosque built on the spot where a delegation of Jinn listened to the Prophet reciting the Quran and embraced Islam.
  • 11. Masjid ash-Shajarah: The site where the Prophet called upon a tree to bear witness to his Prophethood, and it moved across the ground to obey him.
  • 12. Birthplace of the Prophet (Bayt al-Mawlid): The historic home of Abdullah and Aminah in Shi'b Abi Talib, preserved today as the Makkah Public Library.
  • 13. Bir Tuwa (Well of Tuwa): The blessed well in Jarwal where the Prophet (ﷺ) rested, took a bath, and prayed before entering Makkah for the Conquest.
  • 14. Jabal Abu Qubais: The historic peak directly overlooking the Kaaba, from which the miraculous Splitting of the Moon (Shaqq al-Qamar) occurred.
  • 15. Masjid al-Raya: The location where the Prophet planted the military standard of Islam on the day of the Peaceful Conquest of Makkah.
  • 16. Masjid al-Bay'ah (Mina): The secluded valley mosque commemorating the First and Second Pledges of Aqabah with the Ansar of Madinah.
  • 17. Wadi Muhassar: The barren depression between Mina and Muzdalifah where Allah destroyed Abraha and his army of elephants (Surah Al-Fil).
  • 18. Al-Hudaibiyah: The historic valley on the old Jeddah road where the famous Treaty of Hudaibiyah was negotiated and Bay'at al-Ridwan was sworn under the tree.

Part 2: The 23 Blessed Sites of Al-Madinah Al-Munawwarah

  • 19. Masjid an-Nabawi: The heart of Madinah and the Prophet’s Mosque, containing his sacred resting chamber alongside Abu Bakr (RA) and Umar (RA).
  • 20. Al-Rawdah Al-Sharifah: The green-carpeted sanctuary declared by the Prophet (ﷺ) to be a piece of Paradise.
  • 21. The Sacred Mihrab & Minbar: The pulpit from which the Prophet delivered sermons and the crying palm trunk (Ustuwanat al-Hannanah).
  • 22. Masjid Quba: The first mosque built in Islamic history. The Prophet (ﷺ) stated that praying two rak'ahs in Quba carries the reward of a complete Umrah.
  • 23. Masjid al-Qiblatayn: The Mosque of the Two Qiblas where the divine command was revealed to shift the prayer orientation from Jerusalem to the Kaaba.
  • 24. Mount Uhud: The beloved mountain described by the Messenger (ﷺ): "Uhud is a mountain that loves us and we love it."
  • 25. Shuhada Uhud Cemetery: The resting place of Hazrat Hamza (RA) — the Lion of Allah — Mus'ab ibn Umayr (RA), and 70 valiant martyrs of the Battle of Uhud.
  • 26. Jabal al-Rumah (Mount of Archers): The small rocky hill where the Prophet posted 50 archers under Abdullah ibn Jubayr with strict instructions not to vacate their posts.
  • 27. The Seven Mosques (Masjid al-Sab'ah): The cluster of historical mosques marking the defensive trenches and command posts during the Battle of the Trench (Al-Khandaq).
  • 28. Jannat al-Baqi: The cemetery adjacent to Masjid an-Nabawi where over 10,000 companions, the Prophet's wives, daughters, and infant son Ibrahim are buried.
  • 29. Masjid al-Ghamama: The "Mosque of the Cloud" where the Prophet led the Eid prayer and prayed for rain (Salat al-Istisqa) under a miraculous cloud shade.
  • 30. Masjid Abu Bakr as-Siddiq: Historic mosque where Abu Bakr (RA) led Eid prayers during his Caliphate.
  • 31. Masjid Umar ibn al-Khattab: Commemorating the place where Umar (RA) led congregation prayers near Ghamama.
  • 32. Masjid Ali ibn Abi Talib: The site near the southern gate where Ali (RA) offered prayer.
  • 33. Masjid Bilal: Named in honor of the revered first Mu'azzin of Islam, Hazrat Bilal ibn Rabah (RA).
  • 34. Masjid al-Ijabah: The mosque where the Prophet (ﷺ) made three heartfelt supplications for his Ummah, of which two were granted by Allah.
  • 35. Masjid al-Jummah: The mosque marking the exact spot where the Prophet led the very first Friday congregational prayer after departing Quba for central Madinah.
  • 36. Bir Rumah (Well of Uthman): The fresh-water spring purchased by Uthman ibn Affan (RA) from a Jewish merchant and dedicated as a perpetual endowment (Waqf) for Muslims.
  • 37. Bir Ghars (Well of Ghars): A beloved spring whose water the Prophet drank, and from which he requested to be washed after his demise.
  • 38. Garden of Salman al-Farsi: The date-palm orchard where the Prophet (ﷺ) miraculously planted 300 date palms in a single day to free Salman (RA) from slavery.
  • 39. Saqifah Bani Sa'ida: The historical walled garden located just west of Masjid an-Nabawi where the companions gathered to elect Abu Bakr as the first Caliph.
  • 40. Masjid Suqya & Bir Suqya: The well and station where the Prophet reviewed the Muslim army and made Dua for barakah before advancing to Badr.
  • 41. Badr Battlefield Excursion: The coastal plains 150 km southwest of Madinah where the decisive confrontation between truth and falsehood occurred on the 17th of Ramadan (Year 2 AH).

Authentic Islamic Etiquette of Ziyarat

NOOR-E-HARAM tours are strictly grounded in authentic Sunnah practices. Our scholar guides remind pilgrims that Ziyarat is an act of spiritual reflection and gratitude. We instruct all travelers to avoid innovations (Bid'ah) such as touching stones, writing names on walls, or seeking blessings from dirt. The true blessing lies in honoring the sacrifice of the Prophet (ﷺ) and emulating his Sunnah.

Frequently Asked Questions on Ziyarat

How many hours does the full Ziyarat tour take?

The Makkah Ziyarat tour typically spans 3.5 to 4.5 hours in the morning. The Madinah city Ziyarat spans approximately 4 hours, while the specialized Badr Battlefield excursion is conducted as a full-day guided trip.

Are the buses air-conditioned and wheelchair accessible?

Yes. All tours are operated on late-model Mercedes or MAN luxury air-conditioned coaches equipped with luggage compartments for personal wheelchairs, microphones for scholar commentary, and cold water dispensers.

Can ladies visit Jannat al-Baqi and the Uhud Cemetery?

Under current Saudi regulations, women are permitted to view Jannat al-Baqi and Shuhada Uhud from the designated outer perimeter viewing barriers and convey Salam to the Sahabah, while direct entrance inside the inner grave pathways is restricted to men during specific morning hours.
